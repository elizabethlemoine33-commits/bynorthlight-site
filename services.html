How it works

Three steps, in this order

The order matters, because each step earns the trust the next one needs. You will know where we are going before we start. We can change the route along the way, as long as we agree on the destination.

Step 1

A 30-minute Coffee Chat, free

It goes where you need it to. You do not need the tidy version. Bring it as it is, because I am asking for the accurate one. We talk about what is actually stuck, and why. I will take notes, or record the call if you agree, so I can map it all out afterward.

Step 2

A plan we agree on

If it feels like a fit, we book a second, longer conversation where you tell me what is on your plate. I come back with a plan, and we work through it together until we agree on what done looks like. Nothing starts until you are satisfied.

Step 3

The work, starting at about 20 hours a month

I work inside your company, with you, your team, or both, whatever the work calls for. Each month you get a status update, and if the work needs more hours, they are available.

Common Questions

How does an engagement start?
With a free 30-minute Coffee Chat. I take notes, or record the call if you agree. If it feels like a fit, we book a second, longer conversation, and then I come back with a plan we agree on. Nothing starts until you are satisfied.
What does it cost?
We talk about cost once we have agreed what done looks like. The work starts at about 20 hours a month, and if it needs more, more is available. Nothing begins until you are satisfied with the plan and what it will take.
Where do you work?
I am based in Halifax, Nova Scotia, and the work is done remotely, inside your company, with you, your team, or both.
What kind of organisation is this for?
Canadian founders and leaders of small and mid-sized organisations, typically under 50 people, who are at a strategic or operational turning point and need clear thinking they can act on.
Do you work with businesses outside Canada?
Occasionally. I focus on Canadian organisations because they face a distinct set of regulatory, funding, and market conditions, particularly around AI adoption and privacy law (PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25), that general advice does not address well. If the problem is specific enough, geography is negotiable.
