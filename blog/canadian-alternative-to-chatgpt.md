I went looking for a Canadian alternative to ChatGPT this year because it mattered to me. What I found is that "Canadian" is not one label; it's at least three.

## What does "Canadian" actually mean for an AI tool?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>"Canadian" covers three separate questions: who owns the tool, where the data sits, and whose model runs underneath.</p>
</div>

When I say I want Canadian tools wherever possible, there are three questions underneath: who owns the tool, where the data sits, and whose model runs underneath.

> To me, an American tool on a Canadian server is still an American tool.

I wrote about [AI tool data security for Canadian businesses](/blog/ai-tool-data-security-canadian-businesses.html) if you want the longer version of the data question.

## Is there a Canadian alternative to ChatGPT?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>Cohere's North and Compass are priced by getting in touch, and Augure is the one Canadian tool Elizabeth has used. Its quality was very good, but the free limits were frustrating.</p>
</div>

Cohere comes up a lot when I read about AI, mostly on Threads. I had not looked at its site in a while, so I did. The [pricing page](https://cohere.com/pricing){:rel="noopener"} lists North and Compass, both with "Get in touch for custom enterprise pricing." Nothing on the site is built for a person or a small team the way ChatGPT or Claude is.

I have not used Cohere, so that is only what its pages show. It is not an alternative to ChatGPT in the sense most people mean. Anyone can use ChatGPT, for $20 a month or free. There's a democratization of accessibility to AI.

Augure is the one Canadian tool I have used. It turned up through Facebook ads. I used the free version from June, then a short free paid trial that I didn't give a fair go. The quality was very good, and the free limits were frustrating.

I tested it on a real task: a travel plan for a week in Newfoundland. It asked good questions first, about pace and priorities, and then it produced a detailed itinerary with a fuel strategy and moose warnings. The result was comparable to Claude. The questions were more Canadian-centric, and it gave distances in time, not just kilometres.

Augure's own assistant, its website, and its privacy policy disagreed on where the AI runs, so I asked. Its founder replied that Mistral in the EU is only a backup, that Canadian systems come first, and that the policy is being updated.

I have a paid subscription, and my notes are in one place. Claude is an American tool, and I'm a hypocrite just as much as anyone else. It hasn't been worth switching off Claude for me, but I keep my eyes open when I feel compelled to make changes.

I would absolutely use Augure if coding were more part of it. I don't need so much of a ChatGPT; I need a Codex or Claude Code alternative. When that is part of the package and it's affordable, that's when I'll look to make my move.

## Who is Augure for?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>In my use, anyone working with Canadian law. For coding, I would wait.</p>
</div>

If you are doing things in Canadian law, Augure has some great resources. Canadian and Quebec law are already in there, and some of it is very well done. Augure Legal, a separate product for Canadian law firms, [reviews contracts against your firm's playbook](https://augureai.ca/products/legal){:rel="noopener"}. Its page says it is native in Quebec French and English, with Law 25, PIPEDA and CanLII built in, and that the solo plan starts at $149 a month. Augure's site also lists a Code product, which I have not tried. If you need a Codex or Claude Code alternative, that is the one I would look at.

## Should your business switch to a Canadian AI tool?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>Only if it works for your business. Start with a needs assessment, then test the same prompt in both tools and compare. It may make sense to switch one use case and not others.</p>
</div>

I'm not going to tell you to switch. It has to work for your business, and every company has to make its own choices. Treat this like any other tool change and start by getting clear on five things.

- What data will the tool touch?
- Which use cases are you actually trying to cover?
- How many seats, and who?
- What is the budget?
- What does life look like once you've switched?

If it looks like today, only on a Canadian tool, that's okay.

Then test. Same information, same prompt, how does it perform? Run it in both tools and compare. It may make sense to switch one use case and not others. I also wrote an AI adoption checklist for Canadian businesses [here](/blog/ai-adoption-checklist-canadian-businesses-2026.html).

> Name a tool for what it is.
