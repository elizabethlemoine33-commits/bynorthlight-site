<h1 class="doc-title">PIPEDA AI Compliance Checklist</h1>
<p class="doc-subtitle">Six areas to review before using AI tools with personal information in your Canadian business</p>

<div style="background:#F4F5FA;border:1px solid #D4D8E8;border-radius:6px;padding:14px 18px;margin-bottom:28px;font-size:12px;color:var(--muted);line-height:1.65;">
  <strong style="color:var(--ink);font-family:'Jost',sans-serif;font-size:11px;letter-spacing:.08em;text-transform:uppercase;">Note</strong><br>
  This checklist is a practical self-assessment tool, not legal advice. PIPEDA (the <em>Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act</em>) is Canada's federal private-sector privacy law, administered by the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. For the authoritative text and guidance, visit <strong>priv.gc.ca</strong>. Quebec businesses should also review Law 25, which imposes additional obligations.
</div>

<div class="step">
  <div class="step-header">
    <span class="step-number">Step 1</span>
    <span class="step-title">Know what personal information you're collecting</span>
  </div>
  <div class="step-rule"></div>
  <ul class="checklist">
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You have a written inventory of what personal information your business collects — names, emails, payment details, employee data, client files</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You've identified which of that information is processed by or passed to an AI tool</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You know whether that AI tool uses your data to train its models — and you've read the vendor's terms to confirm</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Sensitive information (health, financial, government ID) is handled with extra care and is not passed to AI tools without explicit authorization</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You've documented the data flows: what goes in, what comes out, where it's stored, and for how long</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="step">
  <div class="step-header">
    <span class="step-number">Step 2</span>
    <span class="step-title">Get meaningful consent</span>
  </div>
  <div class="step-rule"></div>
  <ul class="checklist">
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Your privacy policy discloses that you use AI tools to process personal information — and specifies what kind</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Clients and customers are told their information may be processed by AI, in plain language they can understand</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Employees know which of their information (performance reviews, HR files, communications) is processed by AI tools</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You offer an opt-out where the law requires it — individuals can ask that their information not be used in certain ways</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Consent is not buried in a click-through agreement — it's findable and specific</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>If you're collecting data for AI training purposes (internally or via a vendor), this is disclosed separately from general privacy terms</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="step">
  <div class="step-header">
    <span class="step-number">Step 3</span>
    <span class="step-title">Limit use to stated purposes</span>
  </div>
  <div class="step-rule"></div>
  <ul class="checklist">
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Each AI tool you use has a documented purpose — what it does and what data it accesses to do it</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You're not feeding client data into a general-purpose AI tool without a documented business reason</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>If a tool's use has expanded since you first deployed it, you've revisited whether the original consent still applies</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Personal information collected for one purpose (e.g., billing) is not being used for another (e.g., AI model training) without separate consent</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You have a process for reviewing new AI tools before they're added to your stack — someone approves before deployment</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="step">
  <div class="step-header">
    <span class="step-number">Step 4</span>
    <span class="step-title">Know where data is stored and processed</span>
  </div>
  <div class="step-rule"></div>
  <ul class="checklist">
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You know which country each AI vendor processes and stores your data in — US, Canada, EU, or unclear</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Where data crosses borders, you've assessed whether PIPEDA's cross-border transfer rules are satisfied</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>If you have Quebec clients or employees, you've reviewed Law 25's cross-border transfer obligations — they're stricter than federal PIPEDA requirements</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Your vendor contracts include a data processing agreement (DPA) or equivalent that specifies their obligations</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You know what happens to your data if you cancel the vendor relationship — deletion timelines, export rights</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You retain only as long as necessary — you have a retention and deletion schedule</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="step">
  <div class="step-header">
    <span class="step-number">Step 5</span>
    <span class="step-title">Uphold individual rights</span>
  </div>
  <div class="step-rule"></div>
  <ul class="checklist">
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Individuals can ask what personal information you hold about them — and receive a response within 30 days</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>If AI tools have produced inaccurate information about an individual, you have a process to correct it</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Individuals can request deletion where it's legally required — you know when that applies and when it doesn't</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>If AI is making consequential decisions about individuals (creditworthiness, job screening, eligibility), humans review those decisions</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You have a named privacy contact — a person, not just a generic inbox — for individuals to reach with questions</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="step">
  <div class="step-header">
    <span class="step-number">Step 6</span>
    <span class="step-title">Prepare for breaches and incidents</span>
  </div>
  <div class="step-rule"></div>
  <ul class="checklist">
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You have a written breach response plan — not just "call IT"</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>The plan covers AI-specific incidents: model output leaks, unauthorized access via a vendor's API, or training data exposure</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Under PIPEDA, you must notify the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and affected individuals if a breach poses a real risk of significant harm — the threshold and process are at priv.gc.ca</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You maintain a breach log — all breaches recorded, even those that don't meet the notification threshold</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>Your vendors have told you how they will notify you of a breach on their end, and their SLA is documented</li>
    <li><div class="check-box"></div>You review your AI compliance posture at least annually, or whenever you add a significant new tool</li>
  </ul>
</div>
