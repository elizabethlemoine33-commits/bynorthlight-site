What a fractional COO costs depends on what you want done, how long it takes and how it ends. That is why a price means little until it sits next to a scope.

## What do you get from a fractional COO?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>You get someone who works out a specific problem, or a series of them, takes issues and decisions off your plate and does the work.</p>
</div>

A fractional COO comes in and solves a specific problem. Or a series of specific problems. They jump in and actually do the work. They meet your staff, make staffing changes if need be, assign work, and look at what the business actually needs and what is and is not supporting it. They make calls on your behalf. For how the role works week to week, see [what a fractional COO is](/blog/what-is-fractional-coo.html).

They will tell you the truth about what is happening, and then they will work that truth. A good one owns the problem. That means setting conditions so people know who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed, who can decide and action what, with good documentation, and changing the processes so the problem does not come back.

In my experience, consultants often describe the problem back and give you next steps. A fractional COO gets hands-on with the issue and the team and makes real decisions, to ensure the problem is no longer a problem. Both have a place; [how to choose between them](/blog/consultant-fractional-coo-or-full-time-coo-how-to-choose.html) is its own post.

## How is a fractional COO engagement scoped?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>I start with what is making your life as CEO or founder difficult, and turn the answers into a scope of work over a set period.</p>
</div>

This is how I scope it. It is my practice, and other fractional COOs may work differently.

1. List what is making your life as CEO or founder difficult.
2. Match a solution to each item.
3. Turn that into a scope of work over a set period of time, not unlike a consultant, but not consulting.
4. Recommend a bundle of hours against that scope.

The hours can be used in different ways: availability for calls, or taking point on an issue.

I price bundles of hours against a scope. That is how I work, not necessarily how every fractional COO does. [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/services/interim-fractional-executives/interim-fractional-coo/){:rel="noopener"} says fractional COOs are usually paid on a retainer, hourly or project basis, so ask which one you are being quoted.

## How many hours does a fractional COO work?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>As many as the work needs.</p>
</div>

It depends on what is needed. A fractional COO can be there for any amount of time: ten or 20 hours a week, five hours a week, 15 to 30 hours a month. Those are examples, not a standard.

They are not there full-time. They are just there for what you need. They come in, they step in, they do the thing, they step back.

When you compare quotes, check whether you are reading weeks or months.

## What drives the cost of a fractional COO?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>The scope, the hours and the length of the engagement, so read any quote against those and not against a single number.</p>
</div>

Ask each quote these questions:

- What problem, or series of problems, does the scope cover, and what is the outcome?
- How many hours, per week or per month, and are they for availability or for taking point on an issue?
- Over what period?
- How will you know the outcome is reached, and what does wrapping up look like?
- When can you reach them, and by what channel?
- What happens if the scope changes?

For market context, Go Fractional publishes a [COO rate benchmark](https://www.gofractional.com/insights/rates/coo){:rel="noopener"} built from public job posts and candidate profiles over 90 days: 4 job posts and 832 candidate profiles, last updated October 29, 2026.

It reports an average of $192 an hour, a middle half of $150 to $225 an hour, and an estimated monthly retainer of $7,200 to $10,800 at roughly 12 hours a week. The page does not state a currency, and its full-time comparison uses US labour data. It is a benchmark, not a Canadian price and not a quote.

<div class="callout-block">
<p>Fractional can suit a business that cannot afford a full-time COO but needs support.</p>
</div>

A full-time COO means salary, benefits and potentially equity or bonuses. In Canada, [PayScale lists an average base salary of about C$143,000](https://www.payscale.com/research/CA/Job=Chief_Operating_Officer_(COO)/Salary){:rel="noopener"}, before any of those. For more on that comparison, see [fractional COO vs hiring full-time in Canada](/blog/fractional-coo-vs-hiring-full-time-canada.html).

## How do you set availability and on-call expectations?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>Agree up front when you can reach them, on which channels and how fast they reply, and put it in the contract.</p>
</div>

It is not fair to expect anyone to be on call 24/7, and a good CEO wants their leaders rested. A fractional COO may also work for several companies. So define these before you start:

- The hours you can reach them.
- The channels: Slack, WhatsApp, text, email.
- Their response time.
- What is available outside those hours, including weekends.

There is a difference between an emergency and a 2:30 a.m. brainwave. If the building's on fire, you take the call and go in. Be okay with a reply like "I got your message, I will work on that Monday."

## What does the founder still have to do?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>Trust them, give them authority, step back, and keep doing your own part.</p>
</div>

It works provided you trust your fractional COO and empower them. If you do not give a fractional leader authority, you might as well not hire them; the [consultant post](/blog/consultant-fractional-coo-or-full-time-coo-how-to-choose.html) has more on how much to give.

Then step back. You can't be the one that everybody comes to afterwards to complain. Not everyone will like the changes, and the COO builds the relationships and sometimes deals with the villagers with their pitchforks. Let them.

A good COO frees you to do other things, but you still have to do the things.

## How does the engagement end, and what if the scope changes?

<div class="answer-block">
<p>Agree how you will know the outcome is reached, and renegotiate if the scope changes.</p>
</div>

Time and outcome are both part of the ask. Settle what wrapping up looks like and whether you get progress reports. If the work grows past what you agreed, ask to have the contract changed, or the COO says what can no longer be done. I had to learn that one the hard way. Also decide what information they can access and what they sign. The [consultant post](/blog/consultant-fractional-coo-or-full-time-coo-how-to-choose.html) covers all three in more detail.
