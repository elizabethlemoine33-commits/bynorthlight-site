Who we help

Stuck on Consensus

For the leader with a team around them.

At a glance

  • Who it's for: founders, CEOs and leaders of small and mid-sized Canadian organizations (typically under 50 people) who have a capable team, plenty of opinions, and a decision that keeps circling.
  • How it starts: a free 30-minute Coffee Chat with Elizabeth Lemoine; she takes notes, or records the call if you agree.
  • What comes next: if it's a fit, a second, longer conversation, then a plan you both agree on before any work begins.
  • The work: starts at about 20 hours a month, inside your company, with a monthly status update.
  • Where: based in Halifax, Nova Scotia; the work is done remotely.

The thread is forty messages long, and the latest post still asks the question from message one. Everyone has weighed in. Everyone is reasonable. No one is loudly saying no, and no one is loudly saying yes. The roadmap has a slide with a question mark on it, and the next all-hands is already on the calendar.

You have a good team, and you trust them. The facts and the opinions are all in the room. What no one has done is ask the plain question: yes or no? You have spent years making sure you were never the leader who just decides, so you ask for one more round of input, and from the outside it looks a lot like being collaborative. Sound familiar?

What happens if nothing changes?

The cost isn't one late call; it's every call after it. The roadmap stays frozen. The decisions pile up behind the one that is stuck, and somewhere in there you stop being sure whether you are leading the room or just managing it.

The team stops believing that alignment means progress.

What could get better?

Picture walking into the next all-hands with a decision that is actually done: not reopened, not relitigated. Your team knows who owns the next call before it comes up. You are still the collaborative leader you set out to be; you just don't have to wait on consensus to prove it.

Who will I be working with?

I'm Elizabeth Lemoine. I've sat in rooms where a team has all the information and none of the will to act on it, and I've seen what happens when someone finally says: this is the call. I'm not here to run a workshop you'll forget.

I'll say out loud what the room already half-knows, so you don't have to carry "dictator" for doing what nobody else would. Then I make the decision happen, and build the structure so the next one doesn't take three weeks either.

What does working together look like?

Working together starts with a free 30-minute Coffee Chat, then a longer conversation and a plan you both agree on, and then work inside your company that starts at about 20 hours a month.

This works in three steps, and the order matters, because each one earns the trust the next one needs. You'll know where we're going before we start; we can change the route along the way, as long as we agree on the destination.

Step 1

A 30-minute Coffee Chat, free

It goes where you need it to. You don't need the tidy version. Bring it as it is, post-its and all; I'm not asking for the one you'd give your board, just the accurate one. We talk about what's actually stuck, and why. I'll take notes, or record the call if you agree, so I can map it all out afterward.

Step 2

A plan we agree on

If it feels like a fit, we book a second, longer conversation where you tell me what's on your plate: the concerns, the issues, whatever is keeping the list from shrinking. I come back with a plan, and we work through it together until we agree on what “done” looks like. Nothing starts until you're satisfied.

Step 3

The work, starting at about 20 hours a month

I work inside your company, with you, your team, or both, whatever the work calls for. Each month you get a status update, and if the work needs more hours, they're available.
